### Episode 3: Space as the Architecture of Thought
Ancient Greek and Roman speakers could memorize enormous speeches by placing ideas inside imagined buildings and mentally walking through them. The technique, known as the memory palace, reveals a connection between memory and spatial organization. This episode explores why spatial thinking appears to be so deeply embedded in the way humans remember and think.

### Episode 4: Before Words, Gestures?
What if some of the foundations of language emerged from our ability to communicate spatial relationships? Long before writing, and perhaps even before spoken language, humans may have relied on gestures and other spatial cues to make sense of the world together. This episode explores the gesture-first hypothesis and the hidden spatial structures that still shape communication today.

### Episode 5: Different Ways of Knowing Space
The way you think about direction, distance, and location may feel obvious, but not every culture understands space in the same way. This episode explores how different environments, languages, and traditions can produce very different “spatial etiquette”. This reminds us that while humans share the capacity to “think spatially”, the frameworks we use are shaped by culture and personal experience, as well as cognition.

### Episode 6: Babylonian Maps
Long before satellites or coordinates, the Babylonians were putting space onto clay tablets. This episode looks at two Babylonian maps — one considered the oldest known property survey, the other often called the oldest surviving world map — and what they reveal about a society learning to measure, own, and make sense of space.
[Si.427 Tablet](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/ancient-tablet-shows-babylonians-used-pythagorean-geometry-1000-years-pythagoras-180978376/)
[Babylonian World Map](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1882-0714-509)

### Episode 7: Sacred Centers
